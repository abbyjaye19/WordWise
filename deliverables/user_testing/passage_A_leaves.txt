Why Leaves Change Color

In spring and summer, most leaves are green. The green comes from chlorophyll, a pigment that helps the tree turn sunlight into food. Leaves are full of chlorophyll while the days are long and warm.

But chlorophyll is not the only pigment in a leaf. Leaves also hold yellow and orange pigments called carotenoids. All summer, these colors are hidden because there is so much green chlorophyll covering them up.

In autumn, the days get shorter and the nights get cooler. Trees sense this change and slowly stop making new chlorophyll. As the green fades, the yellow and orange carotenoids finally show through.

Some trees, like many maples, also make a new pigment in the fall called anthocyanin. Anthocyanins give leaves their bright red and purple colors. Sunny days and cool nights help trees make more of them, which is why some autumns are especially colorful.

Eventually the tree seals off each leaf where it joins the branch. The leaf dries out, turns brown, and falls. This helps the tree save water and energy until spring returns.
