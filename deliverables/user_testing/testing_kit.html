WordWise · MGT 409 final project

User testing kit

About 25–30 minutes per person. Test with 1 to 3 people. Pages marked Facilitator are for you; pages marked Tester are printed or shown to the person testing.

1. What we want to learn

  1. Time: Does reading take more or less time with WordWise?
  2. Understanding: Do testers answer comprehension questions better with WordWise?
  3. Experience: Is it easy to use? Which tools help most? What is confusing?

With only 1–3 people this is a small, honest pilot, not a scientific study. The report will say so.

2. Who to ask

Good testers (any 1–3)Why
A student who finds reading hard (ideally someone with dyslexia), age 9 or olderThe main audience: the most important tester
A parent of a student who struggles with readingCan judge whether they'd use it at home
A teacher, tutor, or reading specialistCan judge whether the help is accurate and useful
A classmate or friend (fallback)Still finds usability problems

Privacy: don't record full names. Use "Tester 1, 2, 3" and only general details (for example "student, age 15" or "parent"). No photos or video of faces.

3. Before the session (Facilitator)

Start WordWise: in the project folder run python app/main.py and open http://127.0.0.1:8050.
Create a fresh account for the tester (for example tester1) so their saved words and reading time are their own.
Have both reading files ready to upload: passage_A_leaves.txt and passage_B_volcanoes.txt (in deliverables/user_testing/).
Print or open the plain version of the "without WordWise" passage (just the text file in Notepad, or printed on paper).
A stopwatch (a phone is fine), this kit, and a pen.
Turn the computer's sound on (read-aloud).

4. Consent (Facilitator reads aloud)

Say: "I'm testing a reading website I built for a class project. I'm testing the website, not you, so there are no wrong answers. You'll read two short passages and answer three questions about each. It takes about 25 minutes. I'll take notes but won't write down your name. You can stop or skip anything at any time. Is that OK?"
If the tester is under 18: ask a parent or guardian first and have them stay nearby. Say the same thing to the student in simple words.

Record: consent given yes    parent/guardian consent (if under 18) yes

6. Session steps (Facilitator)

  1. Warm-up (2 min). Ask: "How do you usually read things for school? Do you use any tools?" Note the answer.
  2. Part 1: without WordWise (about 6 min). Give the plain passage. Say: "Read this the way you normally would. You can use anything you usually use, like a dictionary. Tell me when you're done." Start the stopwatch; stop it when they say done. Record the time. Then give the matching question sheet (with the passage taken away) and let them answer. Don't time the questions.
  3. Quick tour (3 min). Show WordWise once: Reading settings, Read aloud, tap a word, select text and "Make it simpler," and "Sum it up" with "Check yourself." Don't show anything else. Then upload the Part 2 passage.
  4. Part 2: with WordWise (about 8 min). Say: "Now read this one in WordWise. Use any tools that help you. Tell me when you're done." Time it the same way. Watch quietly and tick the tools they use on the observer sheet. If they're stuck for more than a minute, you may help, but note it. Then give the matching question sheet (WordWise closed).
  5. Survey (4 min). Hand over the survey page.
  6. Wrap-up (2 min). Ask: "What was the most helpful part? What was the most annoying part? If you could change one thing, what would it be?" Write down their exact words.

7. Answer key and scoring (Facilitator only)

Score each answer 1 (correct), ½ (partly right), or 0. Maximum 3 per passage. Score both parts the same way.

Passage A: Why Leaves Change Color

QuestionFull-credit answer
1. What pigment makes leaves green, and what does it do?Chlorophyll; it helps the tree turn sunlight into food. (½ if only one part.)
2. Why do yellow and orange colors show up in autumn?Shorter days make the tree stop making chlorophyll; as the green fades, the yellow/orange carotenoids that were there all along show through. (½ for "the green goes away" without why.)
3. Why might one autumn be more colorful than another?Sunny days and cool nights help trees make more anthocyanin (red/purple). (½ for "the weather" without detail.)

Passage B: How Volcanoes Form

QuestionFull-credit answer
1. What is magma, and what is it called when it reaches the surface?Melted rock underground; it's called lava at the surface. (½ if only one part.)
2. What causes a volcano to erupt?Gases trapped in the magma build up pressure in the magma chamber; when the pressure is too great, magma bursts through cracks. (½ for "pressure" without gas or chamber.)
3. Why do many volcanoes form where tectonic plates meet?Plates pushing together or pulling apart create places where magma can escape more easily. (½ for "the plates move" without the escape idea.)

Facilitator · After all sessions

Send the results

For each tester, send these to your coding assistant (a photo of the notes sheet works, or type them out):

  • Who they are (general description only), passage order, both times, both scores, reader level
  • Survey numbers (6 ratings) and the three written answers
  • Tools used, problems seen, and exact quotes

They will go into deliverables/user_testing.json, and the report's Impact section will be rebuilt with a results table (average time and score with vs. without WordWise, survey averages, and quotes).
