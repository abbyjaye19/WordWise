WordWise closes the gap between who needs reading support and who receives it, at near-zero marginal cost

Situation

About 5–6% of U.S. students are formally identified with a reading-based learning disability, and up to 15–20% of people show some symptoms of dyslexia.

The most effective help, specialized instruction, is expensive and hard to find.

Complication

Most struggling readers never qualify for formal support. They piece together separate tools (text-to-speech, dictionaries, general chatbots) that don't share context, ignore reading level, and can replace the reading instead of building comprehension.

Resolution

WordWise: one workspace on the student's own PDFs, slides, and documents, with read-aloud and display controls, plus six focused AI agents that adapt to each student's level and self-reported struggles and explain text beside the original at one of four reader levels and check understanding with questions.

Comprehension support can be offered to every struggling reader, not only the formally identified, for under $0.06 per student-month even at heavy use.

Six single-call agents sit behind one guard layer, so cost and failure modes stay bounded

Exhibit 4 · System architecture

1

AI only where it adds value. Display, read-aloud, highlights, and folders cost $0.

2

Single-call, tool-less agents. One click = one call (long readings: ≤4 parallel chunks); no loops.

3

One guard layer on every call: budget cap, input caps, <reading> delimiting, model check, logging.

4

One shared, cached prefix (safety + adapt-to-student rules + level guide); level and profile ride in each request.

5

Student data stays local: only a short self-reported profile is sent; tutor setting suggestions are validated in code.

Source: app/services/ai.py, prompts/; per-call costs are measured warm-cache averages (output/tokenomics/summary.json).

Why caching worked

~650-token prompts sat below the 1,024-token caching minimum (0 cached). A shared prefix now bills 89% of input at the 10×-cheaper cached rate. The current app (18 calls: +2 agents and a profile) costs $2.07 per 1,000 sessions; the table uses it.

Scale implication

The $2/day default cap protects a demo; at 10,000 typical users spend is ~$8.28/day, so the cap must become per-student.
