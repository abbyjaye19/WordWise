WordWise

Reading, made to fit you: an AI reading studio for students with dyslexia.

The problem

5–20%

of people have dyslexia (estimates vary: Yale Center for Dyslexia & Creativity; Understood.org)

  • Reading is slow and tiring, so understanding suffers
  • Expert tutoring works, but it's expensive and hard to find (IDA)
  • Students juggle separate apps: read-aloud, dictionary, chatbot, search

Who: students aged about 5 to 30, reading school books, assignments, and lecture slides

What's new

  • One studio for the student's own reading: upload a PDF, slides, or Word file
  • Help next to the original, never replacing it
  • Pitched at 4 reader levels (5–8, 9–12, 13–17, 18+)
  • Builds comprehension: "check yourself" questions, not just a summary
  • Guides homework instead of doing it, plus trusted resources and tutoring sites

Four focused agents, one guard layer

Cost to run

$0.017

per typical student per month (12 study sessions, AI only, measured)

$165

per month for 10,000 students

  • Measured session: $0.00194 → $0.00137 (−29%)
  • How: one shared reader-level guide pushed prompts past the 1,024-token cache minimum, so most input is 10× cheaper
  • Same request from a classmate: $0 (Portkey replay cache)
  • Read-aloud and reading tools: no AI, no cost

Safety: 13 of 13 attacks stopped

A planted instruction tries to get the AI to give fake answers and ask for an address…

…the tutor treats it as text. Other attacks were blocked upstream by Azure's filter.

Also tested: essay request becomes a plan · 988 shown to a student in distress · other accounts blocked · password lockout · spending cap · wrong-model check

Impact

  • Reach: roughly 50–200 students in a school of 1,000 could benefit
  • Money: about $16 a month in AI for 1,000 typical students; points families to free tutoring
  • Time: answers in about 2–5 seconds, right next to the text
  • Real users: testing with students and families (results in the report)

What's next

  • Measure time and quiz scores with real students
  • Tighter answers for ages 5–8
  • Text recognition (OCR) for scanned pages and photos of book pages
  • Host it with per-student limits and a teacher view for shared assignments
